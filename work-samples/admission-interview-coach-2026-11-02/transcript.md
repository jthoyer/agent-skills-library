# Interview transcript — Senior Product Manager, Brightpay (first round, 45 min)

Fictional fixture for the `interview-coach` admission test. Brightpay is a made-up
payments fintech. Interviewer: Dana (Group PM). Candidate: Sam.

---

**Dana:** Thanks for joining, Sam. To start, tell me about yourself.

**Sam:** Sure. So I studied economics at university, then joined a consultancy for about two
years doing operations projects. After that I moved into product at an e-commerce company,
where I was an associate PM, then a PM, working on checkout and the returns flow. Then about
three years ago I joined Kestrel, which is a B2B invoicing platform, as a product manager, and
I've been there since, working across onboarding and payments. I've worked with lots of
different teams, engineering, design, sales, and I really enjoy that variety. I'm passionate
about building products that users love, and I think Brightpay would be a great next step for
me.

**Dana:** Great. Tell me about a time you disagreed with an engineering lead.

**Sam:** Yeah, so at Kestrel we were launching a new invoice reminders feature, and marketing
wanted to announce it at a conference in March. The timeline was tight, so I set up a weekly
sync with marketing, sales and engineering, and we built a shared launch tracker. There were
some different views on the date, so I made sure everyone had a chance to share their
perspective. In the end we all got on the same page and launched in April, and the
announcement went out in a blog post instead. Everyone was happy with how it went.

**Dana:** But what was the disagreement with engineering, specifically?

**Sam:** Honestly, we didn't really have disagreements. The team was very collaborative. I try
to make sure everyone feels heard, so things don't really get to that point.

**Dana:** OK. Walk me through a product you launched and its impact.

**Sam:** At Kestrel I owned self-serve onboarding for small businesses. Activation, meaning a
new account sending its first invoice within seven days, was 31%. I ran twelve user interviews
and found that bank verification was the main drop-off: it took up to three days. I proposed
instant verification through an open-banking provider. Engineering estimated eight weeks, and
we had a regulatory deadline in six. So I cut the multi-currency setup step from the first
release, which I knew would upset two enterprise prospects, and I told sales why before they
heard it elsewhere. We shipped in six weeks. Eight weeks after launch, activation for the new
cohort was 44%, against 31% for the matched cohort before, and support tickets about
verification fell by about half. If I did it again, I'd have brought compliance in at the
discovery stage. We lost a week to a review I could have started earlier.

**Dana:** Nice. Have you worked with regulated payments before?

**Sam:** Yes. Two years on Kestrel's card-acceptance product. I owned our PCI scope reduction,
which took us from SAQ D to SAQ A by moving card capture to a hosted field.

**Dana:** How do you decide what goes on your roadmap for the next year?

**Sam:** So I keep a backlog in Jira, and every two weeks I groom it with my tech lead. I score
tickets with RICE, reach, impact, confidence and effort, and the highest scores go into the
next sprint. I take a lot of input from sales and support, they log requests in a Slack
channel and I triage those weekly. I also look at our bug count and make sure we keep about
20% of each sprint for tech debt. That keeps the roadmap pretty balanced.

**Dana:** Tell me about a time a project failed.

**Sam:** We built a cash-flow forecasting feature at Kestrel. We spent about five months on it.
We did research, we built a beta, we got it to around forty customers. But the market just
wasn't ready for it, and then leadership changed priorities halfway through, and the VP who
sponsored it left. When the new VP changed direction I didn't want to rock the boat, so we
just went along with it and the feature was shelved.

**Dana:** What would you do differently?

**Sam:** I'd probably push for more resources earlier. With a bigger team we could have shipped
faster, before priorities changed.

**Dana:** We're nearly out of time. Any questions for me?

**Sam:** Yes, what does a typical day look like for a PM here?

**Dana:** Good question. It varies, but... [answer omitted] Thanks, Sam. We'll be in touch.
