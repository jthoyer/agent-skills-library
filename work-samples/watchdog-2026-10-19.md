## Step 1. Measure (before)
Count, excluding protected rows:
- total rows; rows with Library role set; rows with a PM topic or SD element set;
- rows per PM topic (01-16, G1-G9, C) and per SD element, counting only Triage = Keep;
- rows where Review due is ticked; untitled rows; rows whose title is a bare URL.

## Step 2. Pick the batch
Take rows where Library role is empty, in this order:
1. top-level hubs (no Parent item, with Sub-items), largest first;
2. the direct children of hubs already classified;
3. everything else, oldest first.
Stop at 25.

## Step 3. Classify each row
Read the title, the URL, and enough of the body to judge (about 500 words).
Library role - pick one:
- Hub: groups other pages; little content of its own.
- Ontology: only the two ontology pages, or one I name.
- Topic guide: teaches one topic in the guide shape (what it is, goal, gaps, proof, best practice).
- Concept: defines one idea or term.
- Method: a framework, technique or how-to.
- Artefact: a template, canvas or worked example of a work product.
- Source: an outside article, post, video, book or course, mostly a link with little synthesis.
- Career record: about my career - CV, job adverts, interviews, ladders, reviews, certifications.
- Prompt: AI prompts or instructions.
- Off-topic: not about product, service design, leadership, ways of working or career.
PM topic - every topic the page genuinely helps with, using the ontology's 16 topics, G1-G9 for the
gaps the ontology names (G9 is the service design craft), and C for career craft. Max three.
SD element and SD artefact - only where the page helps me work with that entity or artefact.
Career level - Foundation (learning the basics), Practitioner (doing the job well),
Senior (shaping other people's work), Lead (setting direction). Pick the level where it matters most.
Use - Reference, Study, Remind, Apply, Interview prep. One or more.
Key takeaway - one or two plain sentences, in British English, drawn from the page. Skip if thin.
Proof test - only for Topic guides and Methods: one sentence on what I could do if I had learned it.

## Step 4. Score and triage
Score each row 0, 1 or 2 on five tests:
- Relevant: serves my PM / service design career.
- Findable: the title says what it is.
- Usable: I could act on it or study it.
- Current: not out of date or a dead link.
- Unique: no better page covers the same thing.
Triage: 8-10 = Keep. 5-7 = Rewrite. Duplicate of a better page = Merge (name the better page in the
run log). 0-4, or Off-topic = Archive candidate.

## Step 5. Schedule
- Topic guides keep their weekly study slot. Do not move it.
- New rows with Use = Study: set Next review to the next free Monday after the topic-guide rotation.
- New rows with Use = Remind: set Next review 30 days out.
- Others: leave Next review empty.

## Step 6. Check against the ontologies

- any concept the ontologies name that has no page (for example Shostack's blueprint, Torres's

the 16 topic guides use: What it is; The goal; Link to the goal; What is missing (first principles,
gaps, proof you have it); Best practice; Relationships; Platform and service lens. Add a
"Service design lens" section naming the SD elements and artefacts. Create it only after I say yes.
