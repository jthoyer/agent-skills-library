# Research → Ideas Pipeline

Seven stages turn a pile of research into a decision and a test. Each stage stacks a skill already
in the library. This skill owns the sequence, the evidence rules, the decision block, the
test-and-learn loop and the write-back. It does not own the thinking inside each stage.

The stages follow the arc of a good decision. Stages 1–3 diagnose. Stage 4 decides. Stage 5
de-risks. Stage 6 communicates and lands the call. Stage 7 learns, and feeds the next run.

## When this fires, and when it hands off

Fires when research material already exists (pasted, attached, or just gathered in this turn) and
the ask is to turn it into ideas, options, a shortlist, a call or a plan. Does not fire for a single
quick question with no corpus behind it. Answer that directly.

If the research does not exist yet, gather it first, then run the pipeline. Do not skip to Stage 2
on a hunch.

If an earlier run on the same topic exists, this is a re-run. Read "Re-runs" below first.

## Before you start: scope and coverage

Spend two minutes. Put these four lines at the top of the parent page.

1. **Decision.** What gets decided, who decides, by when.
2. **Limits.** Budget in AUD, hours per week, hard constraints. All money is AUD. Note the FX rate
   and date on any converted figure.
3. **Fit weights.** The two or three criteria that matter most here. They set the weights in Stage 4.
4. **Coverage plan.** List the source classes the decision needs (forums, first-hand case studies,
   verified revenue data, expert interviews, competitor pages). Mark each Reached or Not reached.
   Name the two hardest to reach. If a class the decision leans on is Not reached, say so on the
   page. Confidence then caps at Medium (Stage 4).

## Stages

**1. Cluster: `issue-tree`.** Break the raw research into MECE groups. A group needs at least
three independent, first-hand sources. Label thinner groups "thin evidence" and keep them apart.
Tag every source with its class and platform. Stage 3 needs that tag.

**2. Frame the job: `jobs-to-be-done-jtbd`, stacked with `customer-insight`.** State the job for
each cluster: *when [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]*. If the idea reads as a
feature or tactic ("sell PDFs") and not a job ("prove I did the research so a buyer doesn't have
to"), send the cluster back for another pass.

**3. Grade the evidence.** Grade every claim, strongest first:
   - **Tested:** your own result from a Stage 7 test.
   - **Confirmed:** three or more consistent accounts from at least two source classes, and at
     least one backed by proof (payout screenshot, platform-verified revenue, third-party data).
   - **Inferred:** consistent accounts with no proof, or with proof from one source class only.
   - **Asserted:** one claim, no proof.

   Self-reported outcomes stay Asserted until something backs them. Three self-reports are not
   proof. Accounts count as independent only if they have different authors and none quotes
   another. The grade travels with the idea. Never round it up for a cleaner story.

   Add one **denominator** line per idea: how many people tried this, and how many succeeded?
   Success stories show it can work. They do not show how often. If the count is unknown, write
   "Unknown".

**4. Cost, score and rate confidence.** Record three real numbers per idea first: cost (AUD), weeks
to first signal, and outcome range (low, base, high, in the unit that fits, with the source). Then
score each idea 1–5 on the criteria the decision turns on (reach, cost, time, fit and viability by
default; adjust to the decision). Always include **speed to signal**: how soon you learn whether it
works, not just how soon it launches. Weight the scores with the fit weights. Add them up.

   Rate confidence separately as High, Medium or Low:
   - **High:** Confirmed or Tested evidence, five or more independent sources, denominator known,
     no blocker, strong fit where it matters most.
   - **Medium:** Inferred evidence, three to four sources, or one blocker with a workaround.
   - **Low:** Asserted or thin evidence, or a blocker with no workaround.

   Cap confidence at Medium when one source class supplies most of the evidence, when a class the
   decision needs is Not reached, or when the denominator is unknown. State in one line what would
   move the rating.

   **Ranking rule:** sort by confidence tier first, then by score inside the tier. Write the rule on
   the page. The evidence grade judges one claim. Confidence judges the whole idea. Keep them
   visibly apart.

**5. Stress-test the leaders: `pre-mortem`, stacked with `red-team` for anything high-stakes.** Take
the top two or three. For each, assume it has already failed and name the most likely reason: a
failure story from the research if one exists, otherwise the most plausible mechanism. This is the
catch. An idea with no catch has not been stress-tested. Then size the worst case on its own. Can
it cost more than the budget or hours in scope? Can you undo it? One line each.

**6. Decide and land the shortlist: `scr-pyramid-communicator`.** Governing thought first: which
idea to back and why, in one sentence. Then the **decision block**: the decision, the decider, the
decide-by date, the recommendation, the main assumption that would reverse it, and a review date.
For each idea not chosen, add one line: *Revisit if [measurable trigger].* Then the ranked list.
Close with what the research could not verify. Never drop that line. A pipeline that never admits a
gap is hiding one.

**7. Test and learn: top pick only.** Turn the pick into a test:
   - **Hypothesis:** we believe [who] will [do what] because [evidence]. We will know when
     [measurable signal].
   - **Cheapest test** that fits the time limit, and the metric it reads.
   - **Decision rule:** one threshold each for Continue, Change and Stop. Check the three for gaps
     and overlaps. Every possible result must land in exactly one outcome.
   - **Review date,** set as the plan page's Due Date.

   The plan page holds day-1 actions, weekly goals with checkpoints, spend, risks and the decision
   rule. When the result arrives, record it as Tested evidence and re-run.

## Re-runs

Before Stage 1, find the latest parent page in Category Research on the same topic and read it. Do
not start over. Link the old page in the first line of the new one. Carry forward what still
stands. Update grades where new evidence exists, re-score, and open with a short "What changed"
section. Mark any idea with a Stage 7 result as Tested and re-rank. Promote parked ideas whose
"Revisit if" trigger has fired.

## Output shape

For every idea carried past Stage 3:

- Name (verb first, plain)
- The job (JTBD form)
- Evidence: grade, source count, source classes
- Denominator
- Numbers: cost (AUD), weeks to first signal, outcome range
- Confidence: High / Medium / Low, one line why, one line on what would change it
- Score and the criteria behind it
- The catch and the worst case (from Stage 5)
- First step (under two hours)

Close with the decision block, the top pick and why, and what remains unverified.
