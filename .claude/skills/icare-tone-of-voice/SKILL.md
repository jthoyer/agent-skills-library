# icare Tone of Voice — Programme Writing Standard

## The governing principle

Every output in this project must hold two registers at once.

**Commercial rigour.** The language of scheme finance, cost drivers, duration mechanics, and regulatory accountability. Numbers are honest about their confidence grade. Claims are graded Confirmed, Inferred, or Asserted — and Asserted is not permitted in final outputs.

**Human seriousness.** The people in these schemes are injured workers, terminally ill patients, catastrophically injured individuals, and homeowners in financial distress. The language keeps this human reality in view at all times. It stays measured rather than sentimental. But it holds the human reality present, particularly in DDC and Lifetime Care work.

icare's own identity — **commercial mind and social heart** — is the clearest single expression of this duality. That balance sits behind every output, even when neither word appears.

---

## Voice characteristics

**Declarative, not hedged.**
Write in plain declarative sentences. "Delayed decisions extend claim duration." Not "delayed decisions may, in certain circumstances, potentially contribute to extended duration." When confidence is limited, say so explicitly using the Playbook grade, then write the surrounding prose with full declarative confidence.

**Active, not passive.**
"Delay extends duration." Not "duration is extended by delay." The cause-and-effect chain must be legible at a glance.

**Short sentences carry weight.**
The most important claims are often single sentences set alone. Use this deliberately. A key finding does not need a qualifier before it and a caveat after it. State it. Then explain it if needed.

**Plain English over jargon — but technical where technical is right.**
PIAWE, RTW, CSP, WPI, MAIV Act — use these without explanation in outputs for scheme practitioners. In documents for executive or cross-functional audiences who are not claims specialists, use plain English first and the technical term in brackets if needed for the record.

**Specific over vague.**
"148,000 active claims" not "a large active claims pool." "Confirmed by the SIRA QBE Nominal Insurer Audit, May 2025" not "as noted in recent regulatory findings." Specificity builds credibility. Vagueness undermines it.

**No superlatives, no puffery.**
Every claim must be evidenced. Skip "world-class," "best in class," "cutting edge," and anything else that lacks a source.

## Structural conventions

**Executive summary first.**
Every substantive document opens with a short section that can stand alone — typically 3–6 sentences. It states the claim, the financial logic, and the implication. The reader should not need to finish the document to understand the point.

**How it works before how much it costs.**
Always explain *why* something happens before asserting *how much* it costs. The causal story survives scrutiny. The number is directional.

**Leverage framing, not dollar precision.**
Financial impact is expressed as Small / Medium / Large / X-Large leverage, or as a directional sensitivity band (e.g. $55m–$111m), not as point estimates presented as fact. The $1,500 weekly benefit rate is currently illustrative and must be noted as such until the actuarial figure is sourced.

**Cross-scheme discipline.**
Each scheme has a structurally distinct cost model. NI mechanics — PIAWE, step-downs, RTW, weekly benefits — must not appear in DDC, HBCF, General Lines, or Lifetime Care outputs. This is a hard rule.

**Confidence grades are visible.**
When The Playbook discipline applies, the grade (Confirmed / Inferred / Asserted) appears inline and stays fully visible — include it every time, even when it complicates how confident a claim reads.

## Register by audience

**Executive / Board**
Short paragraphs. The main point in one sentence. The financial implication in one sentence. No bullet points unless the content is genuinely list-shaped. Confidence grade embedded naturally, not shouted. Example: *"The handover risk is confirmed by the QBE NI audit (May 2025); the two-week saving per avoided handover is directional only."*

**Programme / operational practitioners**
More room to unpack the process and the causal chain. Tables and structured sections are appropriate. The reference packs in this project are the model for this register.

**Discovery / analytical**
Full Playbook rigour visible. Gap registers explicit. Source citations present. Upper bounds flagged when internally inconsistent.

**Journey stories / narrative collateral**
Present tense. Human register dominant without being sentimental. The cost logic sits underneath the story, not in it — the narrative does the work; the financial logic provides credibility context separately.

**Local government / agency practitioners (non-specialist)**
Drop analytical shorthand entirely. Replace with plain-English equivalents. See the word list below. The test: would someone managing a team in a local government agency immediately understand this without a claims background? If not, rewrite before adding the technical term.

## Words and phrases — fixed terms (always use these)

These carry legal or relational meaning and must not be substituted.

| Term | Where it applies |
|---|---|
| **Participant** | Lifetime Care and DDC contexts — use in place of "claimant" |
| **Claimant** or **worker** | NI and TMF contexts |
| **Agency** | General Lines and TMF contexts where the client is an institutional risk manager |
| **Duration compression** | NI/TMF financial framing — the shortening of claim length to reduce weekly benefit cost |
| **Volatility dampening** | Lifetime Care and DDC — reducing unpredictable cost swings rather than average duration |
| **Confirmed / Inferred** | Playbook evidence grades — always inline |
| **The programme** | In external-facing outputs; "this work" is fine in plain-language versions |

---

## Words and phrases — use carefully depending on audience

These are useful for specialist audiences but can exclude or confuse practitioners.

| Specialist term | Plain-English alternative | When to use the plain version |
|---|---|---|
| Structural lever | "a direct control on cost and outcomes" | Local government, non-specialist, narrative content |
| Mechanism | "how it works" or "what actually happens" | Operational or narrative content |
| Behavioural stabiliser | "reduces the chance someone escalates or disengages" | Any non-analytical output |
| Reform risk control | "helps avoid compliance failures when reform tightens" | Local government and practitioner audiences |
| Cost driver | "what makes claims more expensive" | Plain-language documents |
| Directional | "a rough guide, not a precise figure" | Any output where the audience may not know the term |

**The test before using any specialist term:** Would a local government worker — someone managing a team or handling agency risk, not a claims specialist — immediately know what this means? If not, say it plainly first, then add the technical term in brackets only if needed for the record.

## Words and phrases — skip these

| Skip | Why |
|---|---|
| World-class, best in class, cutting edge | Unverifiable |
| Passionate about | icare uses this publicly; it does not belong in analytical or executive outputs |
| It is important to note | Verbose — just say the thing |
| As mentioned above / as noted previously | Reference the specific point instead |
| Significant (on its own) | Always qualify: significant relative to what, at what confidence grade |
| Clearly | If it were clear, you would not need to say so |
| Simply | Condescending in a complex regulatory environment |
| Customers | Use the scheme-appropriate term (worker, participant, claimant, agency, homeowner) |
| Holistic | Vague — name the components |
| Uplift (as a noun meaning improvement) | Fine in icare's own internal language; use the plain form in external or executive outputs |

## Scheme-specific register notes

### Dust Diseases Care (DDC)
DDC requires a distinct moral register that no other scheme shares. The claimant population is terminally ill or facing progressive, fatal illness. This reality must be present in the language. It stays measured — present without tipping into melodrama, and warm enough to read as human rather than clinical-only. But the gravity of the situation — and the timeliness imperative that terminal illness creates — must be visible in every DDC output. Timeliness in DDC is not primarily a cost lever. It is a moral obligation and a reputational risk control. That distinction must show.

### General Lines (Motor, Asset, Liabilities)
General Lines is B2B and institutional. The client is an agency risk manager, not an individual in distress. Harm is operational disruption and institutional financial exposure. Empathy here manifests as responsiveness, accuracy, and institutional confidence — not personal acknowledgement of injury. Keep General Lines outputs in this institutional register; leave NI and Lifetime Care's person-centred language for those schemes.

### Lifetime Care
The claimant is a **participant**. Their need is lifelong. The cost model is about volatility — unpredictable swings in care plan cost — not average duration. Write accordingly. "Shorter claims" is not the right frame. "More stable, better-planned care" is.

### Workers Care
Evidence is thin. Most claims in this scheme should be graded Inferred. Keep confidence proportional to the evidence, and state plainly what is not yet known.

## What this skill does not govern

- Layout and formatting decisions (heading levels, table structures, word counts) — those are set by the specific output type and task instructions.
- The Playbook's evidence discipline — the Playbook is always senior to this voice guide.
- Casual in-thread exchange — this guide applies to substantive outputs intended for programme use, not conversational replies.
