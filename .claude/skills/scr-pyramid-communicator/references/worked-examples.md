## Example 1 — Executive email: recommending a new CRM system

**Context:** Product manager writing to a Chief Operating Officer.

---

**Subject:** CRM upgrade required to recover customer satisfaction — decision needed by 30 April

**Situation**
Customer satisfaction scores have declined 20% over the past two quarters, placing us below our contractual performance threshold for three enterprise clients.

**Complication**
Root-cause analysis points to three compounding factors: our CRM system cannot route tickets by priority, average wait times have increased from 4 to 11 minutes, and first-contact resolution has dropped to 48% against an industry benchmark of 72%. Without intervention, we risk two client contract reviews in Q3.

**Recommendation**
We recommend a two-track intervention: replace the CRM platform with [Vendor X] and hire five additional service representatives, targeting a 30% improvement in response time by end of Q3.

Three reasons support this:

1. **CRM replacement removes the root constraint.** [Vendor X] includes priority routing and automated escalation; our modelling shows this alone recovers 60% of the wait-time gap.

2. **Staffing covers peak-hour demand.** Call volume data shows a 40% spike between 10am and 1pm that our current team cannot absorb; five additional reps bring us to 95% coverage during this window.

3. **The business case is positive.** Total investment is $280K. Avoiding one client contract termination (valued at $400K ARR) makes this NPV-positive in year one.

**Next step:** Please confirm approval of the budget and the hiring requisition by 30 April so procurement can begin vendor onboarding in May.

## Example 2 — Verbal pitch (60-second version)

**Context:** Service designer presenting to a leadership team.

> "Here's where we are. Participant satisfaction with our claims intake process has dropped 15 points this year — below our target — and complaint volumes are up 30%. The core problem is that participants are hitting three friction points in the first 48 hours: unclear next steps after lodgement, no single point of contact, and inconsistent triage decisions. Left unaddressed, this will drive avoidable escalations and reputational risk heading into the review cycle.
>
> Our recommendation is a redesigned intake journey built around three changes: a standard post-lodgement communication template, a named case coordinator assigned within 24 hours, and a triage decision framework for frontline staff. Piloted across two cohorts, this reduced repeat contacts by 28% and improved satisfaction by 18 points. We're asking for approval to scale this across all incoming claims from July."

## Example 3 — One-page brief: proposing a service design research programme

**Situation**
The Workers Care scheme currently serves approximately 1,200 participants with complex, long-term care needs. Service design improvements have been identified as a priority in the scheme's strategic plan, but no structured research into participant experience has been conducted since 2021.

**Complication**
Without current evidence, three risks emerge: design decisions are based on outdated assumptions, improvement investments may not address actual pain points, and the scheme cannot demonstrate participant-centricity to external stakeholders. A 2024 audit flagged participant experience as a material gap in scheme governance.

**Recommendation**
Governing thought: A six-month qualitative research programme, commencing in Q2, will provide the evidence base needed to prioritise and design effective service improvements.

- **Key Point 1: The research scope is targeted and feasible.**
  Three participant cohorts have been identified (newly accepted, mid-journey, and exiting participants); 30 depth interviews and 6 co-design workshops are scoped. Estimated resource: 0.6 FTE service designer + 0.2 FTE research coordinator.

- **Key Point 2: The methodology is fit for purpose.**
  Qualitative methods are appropriate given the complexity of participant journeys and the need to surface unmet needs rather than validate known hypotheses. An independent ethics review will be completed prior to fieldwork.

- **Key Point 3: Findings will directly inform the FY26 investment pipeline.**
  Output will be a prioritised opportunity map linked to the scheme's service expectations framework, ready for the August planning cycle.

**Call to Action:** Approve the research programme and associated budget ($85K) at the April Leadership Team meeting.
