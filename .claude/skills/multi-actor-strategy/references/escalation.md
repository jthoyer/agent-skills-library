# Escalation Protocol and Coordination Principles

---

## Four Situations Requiring Escalation

Escalate to the Project Manager rather than resolving bilaterally between actors in these situations.

### 1. Framing Disagreement
Strategist believes the Problem Framer's question definition is too narrow or too broad to generate meaningful options.  
→ Project Manager decides the operative question. Decision is logged. Strategist proceeds on that basis.

### 2. Option Survivability Dispute
Challenger and Strategist cannot agree on whether a challenged option should be eliminated or retained as qualified.  
→ Project Manager decides. Decision and rationale are logged.

### 3. Risk Changes the Recommendation
Risk Analyst identifies a failure mode that the Decision Architect has not incorporated into the recommendation — and the Decision Architect disagrees that it is material.  
→ Project Manager decides whether the risk is a condition, a qualification, or a reason to reconsider the recommendation. Decision is logged.

### 4. Recommendation Distortion
Decision Architect flags that a communication adaptation has materially changed the qualified form of the recommendation.  
→ Project Manager determines whether the output requires further work or whether the adapted form is sufficient for the audience's decision-making needs.

## Coordination Principles

**Roles are not hierarchical — they are sequential.**  
No actor outranks another within their stage. The Project Manager holds decision rights only at impasse.

**Option generation precedes evaluation.**  
The Strategist completes the longlist before any actor narrows it. Premature convergence is the most common failure mode in strategy processes.

**The preferred option must receive the hardest challenge.**  
The Challenger's job is not balanced — it is proportional to the stakes. The option most likely to be selected should receive the most rigorous scrutiny.

**Stakeholder positions are data, not constraints.**  
The Stakeholder Proxy surfaces what is true about the stakeholder landscape. What to do about it is for the Decision Architect and Project Manager to determine. The proxy does not decide strategy — they inform it.

**A recommendation is only as strong as its stated conditions.**  
A recommendation that conceals its conditions is not honest. The conditions under which the recommendation would change are part of the recommendation itself.

**Speed is a risk variable, not a success metric.**  
The Project Manager's job is to protect the process from deadline pressure, not to accelerate through it. A faster recommendation that has not been stress-tested produces the worst outcome: misplaced confidence.

## Decision Log Template

When the Project Manager makes an impasse decision, record it in this format:

| # | Stage | Actors in Conflict | Issue | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stage 1 | Problem Framer / Strategist | Question scope disagreement | [Operative question stated] | [reasoning] |
| 2 | Stage 2 | Challenger / Strategist | Option survivability — [option name] | Retained / Eliminated | [reasoning] |
| 3 | Stage 3 | Risk Analyst / Decision Architect | Risk materiality — [risk name] | Incorporated as condition / Noted as caveat | [reasoning] |
