# Slide Principles for the Pyramid Principle

## Slide titles as complete sentences

The most common mistake in executive slide decks is using topic labels as titles rather than conclusions. The Pyramid Principle requires every slide title to be a **complete sentence stating the so what**.

| ❌ Topic label | ✅ Pyramid title |
|---|---|
| "Market Share" | "Our market share has declined 8 points in 12 months" |
| "Cost Analysis" | "Three cost categories account for 80% of the overrun" |
| "Customer Feedback" | "Customers cite wait time as the #1 driver of dissatisfaction" |

When a reader skims only the titles, they should be able to follow the full argument without reading the body of any slide.

## Deck structure using SCR + Pyramid

### Recommended structure for a recommendation deck

**Slide 1 — Executive Summary**
Present the full SCR arc in one slide. Three short paragraphs or three labelled sections (S / C / R). The R section should state the Governing Thought. This slide is the entire story at a glance.

**Slide 2 — Situation detail** *(optional; omit if audience knows the context)*
Expand the agreed facts if needed for alignment.

**Slide 3 — Complication detail**
Evidence that the status quo is unsustainable. Charts, trend lines, or risk data belong here.

**Slides 4–N — Resolution (one slide per key point)**
Each slide title = the key point as a complete sentence. Body = supporting evidence. Follow pyramid logic: the title is the argument; the content proves it.

**Final slide — Call to Action**
State specifically what you are asking for: a decision, approval, resources, a next meeting. Include timing.

---

## MECE at slide level

Each slide in the Resolution section should cover a distinct, non-overlapping argument. If two slides are making similar points, merge them. If an important counter-argument or risk is unaddressed, add a slide for it.

A useful test: read the slide titles in sequence. Do they tell a coherent story without gaps or repetition? If yes, the deck is MECE at the structural level.

---

## Slide design principles (brief)

- **One key point per slide** — resist the urge to double up
- **Titles do the work** — body content supports, not leads
- **Data visualisations** — label the takeaway in the chart title, not just the axis
- **No slide should be skippable** — if you can remove it without losing the argument, cut it
