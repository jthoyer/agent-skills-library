# Read the Room

A skill for diagnosing how someone is showing up emotionally and choosing the right communication approach — validation-first (Imago) or analytical (SCR) — based on their state.

## How to Use This Skill

When Justin describes a stakeholder, a conversation, or a message he needs to send:

1. **Diagnose the state** — Identify C-states and T-states from the description
2. **Recommend an approach** — Imago, SCR, or the Hybrid pattern
3. **Coach the execution** — Draft language, structure the message, or walk through the response

---

## Step 1 — Diagnose the State

Read Justin's description of the person or situation. Map what he observes to the state lists below.

### The 8 C-States (Ready for Analytical)

| C-State | Behavioural Signals |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Calm** | Relaxed body language, measured responses |
| **Curious** | Asking questions, leaning in |
| **Courageous** | Open to hard truths, willing to hear criticism |
| **Confident** | Secure, not defensive about their position |
| **Connected** | Engaged, making eye contact |
| **Creative** | Open to new ideas, brainstorming |
| **Compassionate** | Considering others' perspectives |
| **Clear** | Focused, not distracted or overwhelmed |

**Rule: 4+ C-states → Go Analytical (SCR)**

---

### The 8 T-States (Need Validation First)

| T-State | Behavioural Signals |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Testy** | Short responses, irritable tone |
| **Tense** | Tight body language, crossed arms |
| **Tired** | Low energy, checked out |
| **Triggered** | Emotional reaction, raised voice |
| **Timid** | Hesitant, avoiding eye contact |
| **Threatened** | Defensive, protecting their position |
| **Tormented** | Visibly stressed, overwhelmed |
| **Troubled** | Distracted, preoccupied |

**Rule: 2+ T-states → Validate First (Imago), then shift to Analytical**

---

## Step 2 — Choose the Approach

| Situation | Approach |
|-----------|----------|
| 4+ C-states | SCR only |
| 2+ T-states | Imago → then SCR |
| Mixed signals | Imago first — err toward validation |
| Unknown / email / async | Assume T-state risk; open with validation |

---

## Step 3 — Execute

See `references/frameworks.md` for the full SCR and Imago structures with templates and examples.

### Quick Reference

**SCR (Analytical)**
1. **Situation** — Agreed facts: *"We're currently seeing X..."*
2. **Complication** — Why it matters now: *"The challenge is Y, which means Z..."*
3. **Resolution** — Specific recommendation: *"I recommend we do A, B, C..."*

**Imago (Validation)**
1. **Mirror** — *"So what I'm hearing is..."*
2. **Validate** — *"That makes sense because..."*
3. **Empathize** — *"I can imagine you might feel..."*
→ Pause. Let them respond. Then shift.

**Hybrid Transition Phrases**
- "Now that I understand where you're coming from..."
- "Given what you've shared..."
- "With that context, here's what I'm thinking..."

## Coaching Notes

When preparing for a conversation, ask Justin:
- "What have you noticed about how they've been showing up lately?"
- "What happened last time you tried to push a recommendation with them?"
- "Are they under pressure right now from anything else?"

When drafting a message or email, default to opening with validation if the relationship has any friction — logic in writing lands even harder than in person.

Common mistakes to flag:
- Using SCR when T-states are present → they'll get more defensive
- Over-validating and never shifting → the point never lands
- Skipping the Mirror step → they won't feel heard
- Rushing the transition → the shift to C-state needs a beat
