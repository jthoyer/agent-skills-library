# Frameworks Reference

Full structure, templates, and examples for SCR and Imago Dialogue.

---

## SCR Framework (Analytical Approach)

**Use when:** 4+ C-states present. They're receptive to logic, data, and recommendations.

### Structure

**S = Situation**
What's happening? Start with agreed-upon facts.
- What does the audience already know?
- What's the shared starting point?

*Template: "We're currently seeing X..." or "As you know, X..."*

**C = Complication**
Why it matters. Create tension.
- What's the problem or opportunity?
- What are the consequences if unaddressed?
- Why should they care NOW?

*Template: "The challenge is Y, which means Z..."*

**R = Resolution**
What you recommend. Be specific.
- What's the recommended action?
- What are the key steps?
- What do you need from them?

*Template: "I recommend we do A, B, C..."*

### Example

> "We're seeing a 15% drop in user activation this quarter. (S)
> The problem is our onboarding flow has 7 steps — competitors have 3. We're losing users at step 4. (C)
> I recommend we simplify to 4 steps, starting with removing the profile completion requirement. (R)"

## Imago Dialogue (Validation Approach)

**Use when:** 2+ T-states present. They're defensive. They need to feel heard before they'll listen.

### Structure

**1. Mirror**
Repeat back what they said — in their words, not yours.
*Template: "So what I'm hearing is..."*

**2. Validate**
Acknowledge their perspective makes sense. You don't have to agree — just recognise it's logical from where they're standing.
*Template: "That makes sense because..."*

**3. Empathize**
Express understanding of the feeling underneath the words.
*Template: "I can imagine you might feel..."*

### Example

> Them: "We don't have time for another design review. We're already behind."
>
> You: "So what I'm hearing is you're under pressure to ship and worried about adding more process." *(Mirror)*
> "That makes sense — you've got a deadline and the team is stretched." *(Validate)*
> "I can imagine that feels frustrating when someone suggests slowing down." *(Empathize)*
>
> [PAUSE — let them respond]
>
> Then: "What if I told you this review could actually save time by catching issues before dev?"

### Key Rule

Complete all three steps BEFORE presenting your point. The goal is to make them feel heard — not to soften them up for your argument.

## The Hybrid Pattern

The most common real-world scenario: the person starts in T-states, you validate, they shift, then you go analytical.

1. **Read the room** → Detect T-states from their behaviour or the context
2. **Validate (Imago)** → Mirror, Validate, Empathize
3. **Check for shift** → Are they calmer? Are they asking questions? Are they engaging?
4. **Transition** → Signal the shift explicitly
5. **Go Analytical (SCR)** → Situation, Complication, Resolution

### Transition Phrases

- "Now that I understand where you're coming from..."
- "Given what you've shared..."
- "With that context..."
- "Here's how I think we can address that..."

---

## Async / Written Communication

When writing an email or message where you can't observe body language:

- **Default to Imago-first** if there's any existing friction or pressure in the relationship
- Open by acknowledging their situation or constraints before presenting your point
- Use SCR structure for the analytical portion once you've opened with validation
- Avoid leading with recommendations to someone who may be in T-states — it reads as dismissive

**Email pattern:**
> "I know you're deep in [X right now / a difficult quarter / a busy period]... *(Implicit validation)*
> Here's what I'm seeing and what I'd like your input on..." *(Transition to SCR)*
