# Role Guide: Lead, Senior, and Junior

## Overview

On a three-person service design or product team, role clarity prevents both under-contribution and over-reach. This guide outlines the responsibilities, rituals, and development focus for each role.

---

## The Lead

### Core responsibilities
- Holds overall accountability for project delivery, quality, and stakeholder relationships
- Sets the project's operating rhythm and adjusts it as the project evolves
- Manages upward to the manager and outward to stakeholders
- Creates the conditions for the senior and junior to do their best work
- Makes or escalates decisions that are outside the team's authority

### In rituals
- Facilitates weekly kick-off and stakeholder working sessions
- Writes or approves all written updates (status, manager, escalation pre-reads)
- Runs the fortnightly 1:1s with senior and junior
- Is responsible for closing loops with stakeholders

### Relationship with the senior
- Delegates substantive work with clear briefs and outcomes
- Draws on the senior's expertise — doesn't override it unnecessarily
- Provides honest feedback fortnightly; invites the senior's feedback on the lead's own performance

### Relationship with the junior
- Actively creates stretch opportunities each fortnight
- Provides specific, timely feedback (not just praise)
- Briefs the junior before high-stakes moments (stakeholder presentations, senior reviews)
- Watches for signs of overwhelm or disengagement

### Development focus (connecting to PM curriculum)
- Executive communications (8): Writing and presenting to senior stakeholders
- Stakeholder influence without authority (9): Managing competing agendas
- Presence and perceived leadership (12): Showing up credibly in rooms where decisions are made
- Developing your team (14): Growing the senior and junior intentionally

## The Senior

### Core responsibilities
- Delivers high-quality substantive work — research synthesis, design outputs, analysis
- Acts as a sounding board for the lead on approach and quality
- Supports the junior's development through modelling good practice and informal feedback
- Flags risks and issues to the lead before they become problems
- Can run the project for 48 hours in the lead's absence

### In rituals
- Contributes actively to weekly kick-off and retro
- Presents substantive work in stakeholder working sessions
- Participates in fortnightly 1:1 with lead (leads the conversation, not the lead)
- Takes on facilitation where appropriate as a development opportunity

### Relationship with the lead
- Is direct about concerns, disagreements, or capacity issues — doesn't wait to be asked
- Brings solutions, not just problems
- Gives the lead honest feedback when asked in 1:1s

### Relationship with the junior
- Models how to handle ambiguity, feedback, and stakeholder interaction
- Creates informal coaching moments without taking over the junior's work
- Flags junior development concerns to the lead early

### Development focus (connecting to PM curriculum)
- Cross-functional collaboration (13): Understanding and working with adjacent functions
- Giving and receiving feedback (11): Modelling constructive feedback culture
- Team communications (10): Running sessions, writing documentation that gets read
- Strategic thinking (16): Beginning to see beyond the immediate project

---

## The Junior

### Core responsibilities
- Delivers assigned work to the standard briefed, on time
- Asks for help when blocked rather than staying silent
- Brings energy and fresh perspective — doesn't self-censor ideas because of seniority
- Takes ownership of their own development — names what they want to learn

### In rituals
- Participates actively in stand-ups and retros (silence is a signal to the lead)
- Uses fortnightly 1:1 to surface concerns and name development goals
- Presents in stakeholder sessions when given the opportunity — prepares thoroughly

### Relationship with the lead
- Is honest in 1:1s about what is and isn't working
- Asks for the brief behind tasks, not just the task itself
- Seeks feedback proactively rather than waiting for the retro

### Relationship with the senior
- Treats the senior as a learning resource, not just a colleague
- Asks "why" as well as "what" when receiving briefs

### Development focus (connecting to PM curriculum)
- Understanding the product lifecycle (1): How projects move through phases
- Market and user research (2): Core skills for service design work
- Team communications (10): Running good meetings; writing clearly
- Giving and receiving feedback (11): Being open to critical feedback from peers and seniors

## Signs a Team Is Working Well

- The junior can explain the project goal simply and accurately
- The senior is presenting work, not just completing it
- The lead is writing updates, not scrambling to find information before writing them
- Retros produce one concrete change each week, even a small one
- Stakeholders are reading updates before meetings, not arriving cold
- Escalations are rare, and when they happen, they're handled calmly with options prepared

## Signs a Team Is Under Stress

- The lead is the only person with full context
- The junior is only doing admin or low-stakes tasks
- Stand-ups are running long or being skipped
- Status updates are going out late or being written in a rush
- The manager is hearing about risks from stakeholders before they hear from the lead
